\chapter{RTS Smoother}
\label{ch:rts}

This chapter presents an acausal approach to improve the estimate of the speed of the
opponent. Unlike the classical formulation of the Kalman filter, in which the estimate
$\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k}$ at instant $k$ is computed only from the measurements acquired up to
that instant, the approach prescribes the use of future information, i.e.\ of the
measurements acquired after instant $k$ up to a later instant $N > k$, to compute the
smoothed estimate $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|N}$. The main advantage is that, by exploiting the information carried
by the future measurements, the estimate becomes robust to possible mismatches between the
motion model prescribed by the filter and the actual dynamics of the opponent. The drawback, however, is that the real-time estimate is given up, since the
refined state refers to a past instant. In our application this is acceptable, since the
estimate of the opponent is mainly used by the gap control of the planning module, which
regulates the distance from the opponent in terms of time and does not strictly require the
state at the current instant, so that an estimate referred to a past instant but more
accurate can be used instead.

Section~\ref{sec:rts_problem} introduces the smoothing problem and
Section~\ref{sec:rts_formulation} derives the Rauch--Tung--Striebel (RTS) smoother, while
Sections~\ref{sec:rts_implementation} and~\ref{sec:rts_sensitivity} describe its
implementation and present the results. Finally, Section~\ref{sec:rts_qff} proposes its
combination with the feed-forward process noise approach introduced in the previous chapter.

\section{From Filtering to Smoothing}
\label{sec:rts_problem}

Filtering, prediction and smoothing all solve the same problem, i.e.\ the estimation of the
state $\mathbf{x}_k$ at instant $k$ as the value that maximizes its conditional probability
density given a set of measurements $\mathbf{Y}_N = \{\mathbf{z}_1, \dots, \mathbf{z}_N\}$,
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|N} = \arg\max_{\mathbf{x}_k}\; p\left(\mathbf{x}_k \mid \mathbf{Y}_N\right),
    \label{eq:rts_map}
\end{equation}
and they differ only in the set of measurements available with respect to the instant of
interest, which determines how the conditional density is computed.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Prediction}, with $N < k$. The measurements are not available up to
    instant $k$, and the density is propagated from the last available one through the
    transition density of the motion model, so that no new information enters the estimate.
    \item \textbf{Filtering}, with $N = k$. The predicted density is complemented with the
    likelihood of the measurement at the current instant, which, for a linear Gaussian system, is solved in closed form by the Kalman filter
    introduced in Chapter~\ref{ch:tracking}.
    \item \textbf{Smoothing}, with $N > k$. Besides the past and the current measurements,
    also those acquired after instant $k$ are used, and the filtered density is corrected
    with the information carried by the future measurements, which enters through the smoothed
    density at $k+1$, so that the smoothed density at $k$ depends on the one at $k+1$.
\end{itemize}

For the smoothing problem, two formulations have been proposed, which have been proven to
be equivalent and, for a linear Gaussian system, to provide the optimal smoothed state. The
formulation of Fraser and Potter proposes the use of two independent Kalman filters running
in parallel, the first evolving forward in time from the first measurement and the second
backward from the last one, so that the smoothed state is obtained as the fusion of their
estimates. The RTS smoother, instead,
exploits the recursive structure of the smoothing problem, which links the smoothed density
at $k$ to the one at $k+1$, and computes the smoothed estimate with a backward
recursion that starts from the last instant and runs on the output of a single forward
Kalman filter. The RTS formulation is chosen in this work, since it only requires the
quantities already computed by the forward filter to be stored, and it does not need a
second filter running in parallel with its own initialization, the backward recursion
being instead initialized with the estimate of the forward filter at the current time.

\section{RTS Smoother Formulation}
\label{sec:rts_formulation}

The derivation of the closed form of the RTS smoother is presented for the linear Gaussian
system
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{x}_{k+1} = \mathbf{F}_k\,\mathbf{x}_k + \mathbf{w}_k,
    \qquad
    \mathbf{z}_k = \mathbf{H}_k\,\mathbf{x}_k + \mathbf{v}_k,
    \label{eq:rts_linear}
\end{equation}
under the following assumptions, which ensure that all the densities of
Section~\ref{sec:rts_problem} remain Gaussian and are fully described by their mean and
covariance.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Linearity.} The dynamics and the measurements are linear in the state. For
    the EKF of the tracker, the equations are applied to the system linearized around the
    filtered estimate, with $\mathbf{F}_k$ the Jacobian~\eqref{eq:tt_F} of the motion model.
    \item \textbf{Gaussian white noises.} The process and the measurement noises are
    zero-mean, Gaussian, uncorrelated in time and mutually independent, with covariances
    $\mathbf{Q}_k$ and $\mathbf{R}_k$.
    \item \textbf{Markov property.} Given the current state, the next state does not depend
    on the past ones, and each measurement depends only on the state at the same instant.
\end{itemize}

The structure of the smoother is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:rts_scheme}. The forward pass is the
Kalman filter of the tracker, which processes the measurements in chronological order and
stores, at each step, the quantities required by the backward pass. The backward pass is
then initialized at the last step with the filtered estimate and runs in reverse
chronological order, computing each smoothed state from the one of the following step and
from the quantities stored by the forward pass. The two passes are described in the
following.

\subsection{Forward Pass}
\label{subsec:rts_forward}

The RTS smoother is built on top of a forward pass, which is the Kalman filter of
Chapter~\ref{ch:tracking} and provides, at each step, the causal quantities on which the
backward recursion operates. The state is first predicted through the motion model,
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1|k} = \mathbf{F}_k\,\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k},
    \qquad
    \mathbf{P}_{k+1|k} = \mathbf{F}_k\,\mathbf{P}_{k|k}\,\mathbf{F}_k^\top + \mathbf{Q}_k,
    \label{eq:rts_fwd_pred}
\end{equation}
and then corrected with the measurement,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \mathbf{K}_{k+1} &= \mathbf{P}_{k+1|k}\,\mathbf{H}_{k+1}^\top
        \left(\mathbf{H}_{k+1}\,\mathbf{P}_{k+1|k}\,\mathbf{H}_{k+1}^\top + \mathbf{R}_{k+1}\right)^{-1}, \\
        \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1|k+1} &= \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1|k}
        + \mathbf{K}_{k+1}\left(\mathbf{z}_{k+1} - \mathbf{H}_{k+1}\,\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1|k}\right),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:rts_fwd_corr}
\end{equation}
as in~\eqref{eq:tt_prediction} and~\eqref{eq:tt_update}. Both stages are needed by the
smoother. The filtered estimate $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k}$ and its covariance
$\mathbf{P}_{k|k}$ are the causal states that the backward recursion refines, while the
prediction $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1|k}$, its covariance $\mathbf{P}_{k+1|k}$ and the state
matrix $\mathbf{F}_k$ are used to compare the causal prediction with the smoothed state at
the following step and to compute the smoother gain, which weights this comparison. For
this reason, all these quantities are stored at every step of the forward pass.

\subsection{Backward Step}
\label{subsec:rts_backward}

For the backward pass, the main steps of the derivation are presented first, at the end of
which the resulting recursive backward step, implemented in the tracker, is introduced.
Following the principle of maximum likelihood, the smoothed estimates of two consecutive
states, $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|N}$ and $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1|N}$, are the values that jointly
maximize the logarithm of their conditional density given all the measurements,
\begin{equation}
    L(\mathbf{x}_k, \mathbf{x}_{k+1}) = \log p(\mathbf{x}_k, \mathbf{x}_{k+1} \mid \mathbf{Y}_N),
    \label{eq:rts_L}
\end{equation}
which, since $p(\mathbf{Y}_N)$ does not depend on the states, is equivalent to maximizing the
joint density $p(\mathbf{x}_k, \mathbf{x}_{k+1}, \mathbf{Y}_N)$. Splitting the measurements
into those up to instant $k$ and the future ones, and applying the definition of conditional
probability together with the Markov property of the model, for which the future
measurements depend only on $\mathbf{x}_{k+1}$ and the transition to $\mathbf{x}_{k+1}$
depends only on $\mathbf{x}_k$, the joint density factorizes as
\begin{equation}
    p(\mathbf{x}_k, \mathbf{x}_{k+1}, \mathbf{Y}_N) =
    p(\mathbf{z}_{k+1}, \dots, \mathbf{z}_N \mid \mathbf{x}_{k+1})\;
    p(\mathbf{x}_{k+1} \mid \mathbf{x}_k)\;
    p(\mathbf{x}_k \mid \mathbf{Y}_k)\;
    p(\mathbf{Y}_k).
    \label{eq:rts_joint}
\end{equation}
The first factor carries the information of the future measurements and depends only on
$\mathbf{x}_{k+1}$, while the last one does not depend on the states. The second and the
third factors are provided by the forward pass and are Gaussian, with moments given by the
motion model and by the filtered estimate, so that the dependence on $\mathbf{x}_k$ is
confined to these two terms. In the smoothing problem, the
future state $\mathbf{x}_{k+1}$ is replaced by its smoothed estimate
$\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1|N}$, which maximizes the joint density together with
$\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|N}$, and the smoothed estimate at $k$ is obtained by
maximizing~\eqref{eq:rts_joint} with respect to $\mathbf{x}_k$ or, equivalently, by
minimizing the quadratic cost
\begin{equation}
    J(\mathbf{x}_k) =
    \left\|\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1|N} - \mathbf{F}_k\,\mathbf{x}_k\right\|^2_{\mathbf{Q}_k^{-1}}
    + \left\|\mathbf{x}_k - \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k}\right\|^2_{\mathbf{P}_{k|k}^{-1}},
    \label{eq:rts_cost}
\end{equation}
whose first term weights the mismatch between the smoothed state at $k+1$ and its prediction
from $\mathbf{x}_k$, and whose second term weights the distance from the filtered estimate.
Setting the gradient of $J$ to zero and applying the matrix inversion lemma, as detailed in
Appendix~\ref{app:rts}, the backward step of the smoother is obtained,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \mathbf{C}_k &= \mathbf{P}_{k|k}\,\mathbf{F}_k^\top\,\mathbf{P}_{k+1|k}^{-1}, \\
        \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|N} &= \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k}
        + \mathbf{C}_k\left(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1|N} - \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1|k}\right), \\
        \mathbf{P}_{k|N} &= \mathbf{P}_{k|k}
        + \mathbf{C}_k\left(\mathbf{P}_{k+1|N} - \mathbf{P}_{k+1|k}\right)\mathbf{C}_k^\top,
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:rts_backward}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{C}_k$ is the smoother gain and the covariance follows from the propagation of
the estimation errors through the update of the state. At the last instant, the measurements
used by the filter and by the smoother coincide, so that $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{N|N}$ and
$\mathbf{P}_{N|N}$ are also the smoothed estimates, and~\eqref{eq:rts_backward} is applied
backward for $k = N-1, \dots, 0$, using at each step the smoothed estimate computed at the
previous one and the quantities stored by the forward pass.

The correction applied by the smoother to the causal state is determined by two factors.
The first is the mismatch $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1|N} - \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1|k}$ between the
smoothed state and the causal prediction at $k+1$, which quantifies the information
contributed by the future measurements beyond what the motion model can predict. It remains
small as long as the model describes the dynamics of the opponent correctly, and it grows when
the dynamics changes, e.g.\ at the beginning of a maneuver, when the subsequent measurements
reveal an evolution that the prediction could not anticipate. The second is the gain
$\mathbf{C}_k = \mathbf{P}_{k|k}\mathbf{F}_k^\top\mathbf{P}_{k+1|k}^{-1}$, which weights this
mismatch according to the uncertainty of the forward filter, i.e.\ to the ratio between the
covariance of the filtered estimate and the one of the prediction. A filtered estimate with a
small covariance is therefore only marginally corrected, whereas an uncertain one is pulled
more strongly towards the information carried by the future measurements. The same
mechanism acts on the covariance. At the last instant the smoothed and the filtered
covariances coincide, and, if the smoothed covariance at $k+1$ does not exceed the filtered
one, it does not exceed the predicted one either, since the correction step of the filter
reduces the covariance, so that $\mathbf{P}_{k+1|N} \preceq \mathbf{P}_{k+1|k+1} \preceq
\mathbf{P}_{k+1|k}$. The term
$\mathbf{C}_k(\mathbf{P}_{k+1|N} - \mathbf{P}_{k+1|k})\mathbf{C}_k^\top$
in~\eqref{eq:rts_backward} is negative semidefinite, and by induction backward in time
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{P}_{k|N} \preceq \mathbf{P}_{k|k}, \qquad k = 0, \dots, N,
    \label{eq:rts_cov_bound}
\end{equation}
so that the smoothed estimate is never more uncertain than the filtered one, and its
uncertainty is reduced more where the forward filter is less confident and the future
measurements bring more information.

Finally, recalling from Section~\ref{sec:rts_problem} that filtering and smoothing solve the
same estimation problem, it can be noted that the backward step has the same structure as the
correction step of the Kalman filter, as summarized in Table~\ref{tab:kf_rts}. In the filter, the gain $\mathbf{K}_{k+1}$
corrects the predicted state with the measurement $\mathbf{z}_{k+1}$, related to the state
through $\mathbf{H}_{k+1}$ with an uncertainty $\mathbf{R}_{k+1}$. In the smoother, the gain
$\mathbf{C}_k$ corrects the filtered state with the smoothed state at $k+1$, which acts as a
pseudo-measurement related to $\mathbf{x}_k$ through the motion model $\mathbf{F}_k$ with an
uncertainty $\mathbf{Q}_k$, and in both cases the same gain also corrects the covariance of
the estimate.

\section{Implementation}
\label{sec:rts_implementation}
\label{subsec:rts_window}

Depending on which states are refined and on when the smoothed estimate is computed, three
classes of smoothers are distinguished. A fixed-point smoother refines the estimate of a
single instant of interest as new measurements arrive. A fixed-interval smoother computes
the estimates of all the states of an interval once all its measurements have been acquired,
which is the problem solved by the formulation of Section~\ref{sec:rts_formulation}. A
fixed-lag smoother, finally, provides at each instant the estimate of the state at a fixed
delay behind the current time, i.e.\ $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k-L|k}$ with a constant lag $L$. In
the tracker, the measurements keep arriving and a past but refined estimate has to be
available at every publication, so that a fixed-lag formulation is adopted, obtained by
applying the fixed-interval RTS recursion on a window that slides with the current time.

The smoother is implemented on a sliding window of fixed duration $T_w$, so that, from the
current instant $t_N$, i.e.\ the time of the most recent entry of the history of the tracker,
it smooths backward in time over the interval $[t_N - T_w,\, t_N]$, using all the measurements
contained in it. This choice is preferred to a formulation that fixes the information used by
the smoother, i.e.\ the number of measurements, for two reasons. First, the time span covered
by a fixed number of measurements would change with the configuration of the detections,
being short when the opponent is observed by all the sensors and longer when only some of them
are available. Second, the estimate is used by the gap control, which regulates the distance
from the opponent in terms of time, and, since the smoother replaces a real-time estimate with
one referred to the past, it is preferable to bound how old the smoothed information can be
rather than how much information is used to compute it.

At every publication, the backward step of Section~\ref{subsec:rts_backward} is applied over
the window, as reported in Algorithm~\ref{alg:rts}. The recursion is initialized at the most
recent entry, whose filtered estimate is also the smoothed one, and proceeds towards the oldest
entry of the window, computing each smoothed state from the one obtained at the following entry
and from the filtered state, the prediction and the Jacobian stored in the history by the
forward pass. The smoothed states are then published on the history used by the planning
module, which therefore contains, at the most recent instant, the causal prediction of the
filter and, from the first available measurement backward, the smoothed states.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{RTS smoother on the sliding window of the history, for a single track.}
\label{alg:rts}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE entries $k = 0, \dots, N$ of the history of the track in the window
$[t_N - T_w,\, t_N]$, storing $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k}$, $\mathbf{P}_{k|k}$,
$\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1|k}$, $\mathbf{P}_{k+1|k}$ and $\mathbf{F}_k$
\ENSURE smoothed estimates $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|N}$ and $\mathbf{P}_{k|N}$
\IF{the smoother is disabled \OR $N < 1$}
    \RETURN the causal estimates
\ENDIF
\STATE $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{N|N},\, \mathbf{P}_{N|N} \leftarrow$ filtered estimate \COMMENT{initialization}
\FOR{$k = N-1$ \textbf{down to} $0$}
    \IF{the prediction $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1|k}$, $\mathbf{P}_{k+1|k}$ is stored}
        \STATE $\mathbf{C}_k \leftarrow \mathbf{P}_{k|k}\,\mathbf{F}_k^\top\,\mathbf{P}_{k+1|k}^{-1}$
        \STATE $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|N} \leftarrow \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k} + \mathbf{C}_k\left(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1|N} - \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1|k}\right)$
        \STATE $\mathbf{P}_{k|N} \leftarrow \mathbf{P}_{k|k} + \mathbf{C}_k\left(\mathbf{P}_{k+1|N} - \mathbf{P}_{k+1|k}\right)\mathbf{C}_k^\top$
    \ELSE
        \STATE $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|N},\, \mathbf{P}_{k|N} \leftarrow$ filtered estimate \COMMENT{causal fallback}
    \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\RETURN $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|N}$, $\mathbf{P}_{k|N}$ for $k = 0, \dots, N$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Degenerate cases, such as a track that has just been initialized or a state without future
support, fall back to the causal estimate, so that the output is always complete. Moreover,
since the smoother only writes on the published history and leaves the estimates of the
forward filter unchanged, it is also robust to the out-of-sequence measurements
(Section~\ref{subsec:tt_oosm}). When a late measurement arrives, the forward filter inserts
it according to its acquisition time and reprocesses the following entries, updating the
stored filtered states, predictions and Jacobians, so that the measurement is automatically
included in the next backward pass without any smoothed state having to be corrected.

\section{Sensitivity Analysis and Results}
\label{sec:rts_sensitivity}

Since the smoother maximizes the density of the state conditioned on the measurements
$\mathbf{Y}_N$, the parameter that most influences the quality of the smoothing is $N$, i.e.\
the number of measurements acquired after the instant of interest, which in our
implementation is set by the window length $T_w$. In the following, a sensitivity analysis
on the window length is therefore carried out, to quantify its effect on the smoothed
estimate and to identify its optimal value. The analysis is performed on the speed of the
opponent, compared with the ground truth through two metrics.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RMSE of the speed}. Denoting by $\hat{v}_i(T_w)$ the speed estimated at
    the instant $t_i$ with a window $T_w$, i.e.\ smoothed with the measurements acquired up
    to $t_i + T_w$, and by $v_i$ the speed of the ground truth interpolated at the same
    instant, the error is summarized as
    \begin{equation}
        \mathrm{RMSE}(T_w) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\left(\hat{v}_i(T_w) - v_i\right)^2},
        \label{eq:rts_rmse}
    \end{equation}
    where the $M$ instants are the same for all the windows, so that the values are directly
    comparable. The RMSE is computed both on the whole session (global RMSE) and on the
    instants within the braking maneuvers (local RMSE).
    \item \textbf{Maneuver delay}. The braking maneuvers are identified on the ground truth of
    the opponent, and the onset of each of them is flagged at the instant in which the
    magnitude of its acceleration exceeds a threshold. The same instant is then searched on
    the speed estimated with each window, and the maneuver delay is the difference between
    the two. A positive value indicates that the estimate starts the maneuver late, a
    negative one that it anticipates it, and the delay is summarized by its median over the
    braking maneuvers of the session.
\end{itemize}

The analysis is carried out on the session recorded on 5 September 2026 at Imola.

\subsection*{Sensitivity Analysis with All Sensors Available}

First, the performance of the smoother is analyzed when all the sensors are available, i.e.\
with the position measurements of the LiDARs, of the camera and of the RaDAR, and with the
range rate measurement of the RaDAR. Fig.~\ref{fig:rts_lag_radar} shows the performance of
the smoother on the estimate of the speed of the opponent, comparing the one-step-ahead
prediction of the filter, the causal state, i.e.\ the most recent filtered estimate, and the
smoothed states obtained with an increasing amount of future information, up to a window of
\SI{500}{ms}. As the window increases, the smoothed estimate moves closer to the ground
truth, while the curves obtained with windows longer than about \SI{200}{ms} are almost
superimposed.

The RMSE and the maneuver delay as a function of the window length are shown in
Fig.~\ref{fig:rts_metrics_radar} and reported in Table~\ref{tab:rts_radar}. The causal state is
the baseline of the analysis. It is more accurate than the one-step-ahead prediction, since it
integrates the latest measurements, including the range rate of the RaDAR, with a global RMSE
of \SI{0.938}{m/s} against \SI{1.177}{m/s}. With respect to this baseline, the smoother reduces
the global and the local RMSE by \SI{31}{\percent} and \SI{35}{\percent}, respectively, a
reduction that grows with the window up to about \SI{300}{ms} and then saturates.

This saturation is consistent with the formulation of the smoother. In the backward
step~\eqref{eq:rts_backward}, the correction of each state is weighted by the gain
$\mathbf{C}_k = \mathbf{P}_{k|k}\mathbf{F}_k^\top\mathbf{P}_{k+1|k}^{-1}$, which depends on the
ratio between the filtered and the predicted covariance. With the range rate of the RaDAR, the
speed is directly observed and its filtered variance is small compared with the process noise
accumulated in the prediction, so that the gain is small and the contribution of each future
measurement is rapidly attenuated as it is propagated backward. As a consequence, only the
measurements acquired within about \SI{300}{ms} after each instant affect the smoothed state,
while the later ones bring no further benefit.

\subsection*{Sensitivity Analysis without Speed Measurements}
\label{subsec:rts_radar}

The analysis is then repeated without the RaDAR, so that the speed is no longer measured and
is reconstructed by the forward filter from the position measurements only.

The results are reported in Fig.~\ref{fig:rts_metrics_noradar} and in
Table~\ref{tab:rts_noradar}. The one-step-ahead prediction has a global RMSE of
\SI{2.056}{m/s}, which is reduced to \SI{1.533}{m/s} by the causal state, again the baseline
of the analysis. Without the range rate, the filtered variance of the speed is larger, the
gain $\mathbf{C}_k$ increases and the future measurements are propagated further back in
time, so that the reduction of the RMSE reaches \SI{73}{\percent} and saturates only at about
\SI{600}{ms}.

Table~\ref{tab:rts_noradar} also shows that the smoothed estimate anticipates the maneuvers,
with an anticipation with respect to the ground truth that grows with the window up to about
\SI{500}{ms}, where it saturates at about \SI{-106}{ms}. The same behavior is visible in the
local RMSE, computed on the braking maneuvers, which reaches its minimum of \SI{0.669}{m/s} at
about \SI{500}{ms} and then increases, up to \SI{0.820}{m/s} at \SI{981}{ms}.

This behavior is a direct consequence of how the future information is propagated backward by
the smoother. From the backward step~\eqref{eq:rts_backward}, a variation of the smoothed
state at $k+1$ is transferred to the one at $k$ through the gain,
$\partial\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|N}/\partial\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1|N} = \mathbf{C}_k$, and applying
the step recursively, a variation of the state at $k+m$ reaches the state at $k$ through the
product of the gains,
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|N}}{\partial \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k+m|N}} =
    \mathbf{C}_k\,\mathbf{C}_{k+1} \cdots \mathbf{C}_{k+m-1}.
    \label{eq:rts_memory}
\end{equation}
The information carried by a measurement is therefore not confined to the state that
immediately precedes it, but it is propagated backward to all the earlier states of the
window, attenuated at each step by the gain. When the opponent starts braking, the first
measurements that reveal the deceleration correct not only the preceding state but, through
the product~\eqref{eq:rts_memory}, also the earlier ones, which start to decelerate before
the opponent actually does, so that the onset of the braking is anticipated. The extent of
this anticipation is set by how slowly the product of the gains decays. Without the range
rate the gains are larger and the deceleration is propagated further back in time, while with
the RaDAR the product decays rapidly and the anticipation remains limited.

\section{Combination with the Q Feed-Forward}
\label{sec:rts_qff}

As the last section of this chapter, the feed-forward approach of Chapter~\ref{ch:qff} is
enhanced with the RTS smoother. The motivation comes from the conclusions drawn at the end of
that chapter, where the approach, although correct in principle, was shown to be limited by the
quality of the jerk estimator, which inherits the delay and the noise of the estimate from which
it is derived. Any causal estimator of the jerk is in fact bound to a trade-off between noise and
delay, since the differentiation amplifies the high-frequency noise of the acceleration and the
low-pass filtering required to contain it introduces a phase lag, and this trade-off is
transferred to the maneuver flag in the form of late and spurious activations.

The smoother comes in handy for this purpose since, as shown in the previous sections and
following the formulation of Section~\ref{subsec:rts_backward}, it addresses both limits. It
reduces the delay of the estimate by exploiting the future information, so that the delay
accumulated by the feed-forward estimate before a late activation of the flag can be recovered,
and it attenuates the noise due to spurious measurements and activations by averaging each state
with the adjacent ones through the backward recursion. The two methods are also complementary
from the implementation point of view. As described in Section~\ref{sec:rts_implementation},
the smoother operates on the estimate published on the history, which is the one of the filter
with the feed-forward process noise. The history therefore contains the estimate obtained with
the nominal process noise when the flag is inactive and the one obtained with the increased
process noise when it is active, so that the smoother is directly applied to the feed-forward
estimate, with the same window and without any modification to the tracking module.

The combination is evaluated with the tuning obtained in the previous sections and chapters,
i.e.\ a window of \SI{300}{ms} for the smoother, thresholds $j_{\mathrm{on}} = \SI{15}{m/s^3}$
and $j_{\mathrm{off}} = \SI{5}{m/s^3}$ for the maneuver flag and an inflation factor
$\lambda = 100$. Figs.~\ref{fig:rts_qff_2} and~\ref{fig:rts_qff_4} compare, over two sequences
of corners, the causal estimate with the feed-forward process noise, the smoothed estimate of
the nominal filter and the smoothed estimate of the filter with the feed-forward process noise.

In both figures, the combination removes the two limits of the feed-forward estimate identified
in Chapter~\ref{ch:qff}. The delay accumulated before the late activations of the flag, e.g.\ at
about \SI{1064}{s}, \SI{1078}{s} and \SI{1086}{s} in Fig.~\ref{fig:rts_qff_4}, is recovered, so
that the onset of the braking phases in the smoothed estimate is aligned with the ground truth,
while the spikes due to the spurious activations, e.g.\ at about \SI{1066}{s} and
\SI{1068}{s}, and the possible overshoots of the feed-forward estimate are averaged with the
adjacent states and no longer appear in the smoothed estimate. The smoother applied to the
nominal filter, instead, still presents a delay with respect to the ground truth in the braking
phases of the cases shown. The difference lies in the causal estimate on which the backward
recursion operates. With the feed-forward process noise, the smoother starts from a causal
estimate that is more representative of the actual dynamics of the opponent than the one of the
filter with the nominal tuning, and the smoothing further improves it, bringing it closer to the
ground truth.
