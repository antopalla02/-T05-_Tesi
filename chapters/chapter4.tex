\section{Datasets}
\label{sec:datasets}

The analyses presented in this thesis are based on two on-track sessions, recorded on
26 July 2026 and on 3 August 2026 and referred to in the following as session 0726 and
session 0803. Both sessions took place on the same circuit, the Autodromo Internazionale
Enzo e Dino Ferrari in Imola, a road course of about \SI{4.9}{km} that alternates long
straights, fast corners and chicanes; its layout is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:imola}.
The two sessions have been chosen because, as highlighted in the following, they show
complementary characteristics, both in the dynamics of the opponent and in the relative
geometry between the two vehicles.The main characteristics of the two sessions are
summarized in Table~\ref{tab:sessions}.

\paragraph{Opponent dynamics.} Session 0726 begins with about \SI{500}{s} of runs at an
almost constant speed of about \SI{90}{km/h}, followed by a series of stints at higher
speed, up to \SI{160}{km/h}, separated by different safe stops. Session 0803 consists instead of
continuous laps, in which braking and acceleration phases alternate along the whole
session, overall less safe stops are performed. Table~\ref{tab:ds_dyn} reports the median and the 95th and 99th percentiles of the
speed $v$ of the opponent, together with the 95th and 99th percentiles of its deceleration in braking ($-a_x$, for $a_x < 0$), of its acceleration in
traction ($a_x$, for $a_x > 0$) and of the absolute value of its lateral acceleration
$a_y = v\,\dot\psi$, computed over the samples in which it is moving
($v > \SI{2}{m/s}$).

\paragraph{Relative geometry.} The two sessions also differ in the relative position of
the two vehicles. In session 0726 the opponent is ahead of the ego vehicle for most of
the time (\SI{72}{\percent}), and the two cars swap position only once. In session 0803
the ego vehicle leads for most of the session, the opponent being ahead only for
\SI{35}{\percent} of the time, and the two cars swap position seven times at close
range. Table~\ref{tab:ds_geom} reports the median and the 95th and 99th percentiles of
the range $\rho$ and of the absolute value of the range rate $\dot\rho$, together with the
median lateral position $y_{rel}$ of the opponent in the ego frame. In both sessions the median range is about
\SI{50}{m}, and the range rate is below about \SI{17}{m/s} for \SI{95}{\percent} of the
time.

\paragraph{Sensor coverage.} Fig.~\ref{fig:ds_pd} shows, for each sensor, the detection
rate of the opponent in three range bands, computed separately for the intervals in
which the opponent is ahead of and behind the ego vehicle. A detection is associated with
the opponent when its distance from the ground truth, evaluated at the acquisition time
of the detection, is smaller than \SI{5}{m}. In the two sessions the camera pipeline processes
only the images of the front cameras, so the detection rate of the camera is reported
only for the opponent ahead.

When the opponent is ahead, the distribution of the detections over the range shows
that, within \SI{75}{m}, all the sensors detect the opponent consistently. As expected,
beyond \SI{100}{m} the detection rate of the two LiDAR pipelines drops sharply, while
that of the RaDAR and of the camera remains more consistent up to \SI{150}{m}. When the
opponent is behind, the LiDAR detections are limited to the first range band, because of
the blind spot caused by the rear wing (Section~\ref{subsec:lidar}), and the opponent
is detected mainly by the RaDAR, which keeps a detection rate similar to the one obtained with the opponent ahead.

\section{Ground Truth Reconstruction}
\label{sec:gt}

The evaluation of the measurements and of the target tracking algorithm requires the
true state of the opponent at every instant. Only with this reference it is possible to
quantify the error of the raw detections of each sensor and the error of the estimates
produced by the filter, separately for each component of the state.

The reference for the opponent state is the state estimated on board the opponent
vehicle itself. During the sessions, each team records the output of its own state estimation,
based on RTK-GNSS receivers and inertial units, and shares it after the session. The
data provided by the opponent contain, for each sample, the timestamp, the position and
the heading of the vehicle, its longitudinal speed and, when available, its
longitudinal acceleration, and they are converted into a common structure compatible
with the logs of the ego vehicle. The conversion consists in expressing the position
and the heading of the opponent in the map frame of the ego vehicle; in associating each sample of the opponent with the
sample of the ego vehicle with the closest timestamp; and in low-pass filtering the raw
inertial measurement of the longitudinal acceleration or, when it is not available, in
deriving the acceleration from the speed.

It is important to note that this reference is not exact: it is the output of the
state estimation of the opponent, and it may therefore contain errors related to the
quality of its estimate. Since the errors of the sensors are computed with respect
to this reference, the analyses of the measurement errors carried out in
Chapter~\ref{ch:adaptive_r} are also affected by the accuracy of the ground truth, and part of what is observed as
an error of the detections may originate from the reference itself.
